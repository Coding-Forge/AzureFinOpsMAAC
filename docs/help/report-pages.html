Page-by-page guide

Start here

Report Landing

What it is for: Introduces the report and helps users navigate.

Why it was created: New users need a simple entry point before seeing detailed cost data.

Who uses it: Everyone.

Answers: What is this report? Where should I go next?

Executive

FinOps MACC vs Retail Overview

What it is for: Shows actual Azure cost compared to estimated public retail cost.

Why it was created: Customers need a fast way to understand directional value from negotiated pricing and MACC-related benefits.

Who uses it: Executives, account teams, FinOps leads.

Answers: What did we spend? What would retail have looked like? What is the estimated savings?

Executive

MACC Commitment Overview

What it is for: Shows agreement health, commitment utilization, remaining commitment, expected burn, and pacing.

Why it was created: MACC discussions require agreement-level governance, not only service-level cost details.

Who uses it: Executives, account teams, customer sponsors, FinOps leads.

Answers: Are we on track? How much commitment remains? What monthly run rate is needed?

Trend

MACC Benefit Ledger

What it is for: Tracks monthly actual cost, retail equivalent cost, estimated savings, eligible spend, review spend, and match coverage.

Why it was created: Customers need a durable, audit-friendly history of MACC value over time.

Who uses it: FinOps teams, finance teams, account teams.

Answers: How are benefits trending? Which services contribute most? How much spend needs review?

Investigation

Decomposition Trees

What it is for: Breaks cost and savings down by service, region, subscription, and other drivers.

Why it was created: Users need a guided way to discover what is driving changes.

Who uses it: FinOps analysts, cloud operations, customer engineers.

Answers: What is driving spend? Which services or regions explain the largest changes?

Evidence

Agreement Price Audit

What it is for: Shows row-level evidence behind retail price matching and savings estimates.

Why it was created: Savings claims must be explainable and defensible.

Who uses it: Analysts, engineers, finance reviewers, customer stakeholders who ask for calculation detail.

Answers: Which retail price was used? What meter and SKU matched? How was estimated savings calculated?

Quality

Coverage and Exceptions

What it is for: Prioritizes unmatched rows, multiple-candidate matches, and eligibility review items.

Why it was created: Users need to know where savings estimates are strong and where review is required.

Who uses it: FinOps teams, data quality owners, analysts.

Answers: Can we trust the match coverage? What should be reviewed before sharing savings externally?

Governance

Billing and Ownership

What it is for: Connects export traceability, invoice reconciliation, and ownership/accountability mapping.

Why it was created: Monthly governance requires both numbers and owners.

Who uses it: Finance, FinOps, service owners, governance teams.

Answers: Which run produced these numbers? Do invoice totals reconcile? Who owns follow-up?

Reference

Azure Retail Reference

What it is for: Provides a snapshot of Azure Retail Prices data used for comparisons.

Why it was created: Users need a source reference for retail prices, meters, services, and regions.

Who uses it: Analysts and engineers.

Answers: What public retail price data is available? Which meter, SKU, or region is being referenced?

Validation

Cost Export Validation

What it is for: Checks whether expected export files and row counts are present.

Why it was created: Users need confidence that the report is based on a complete run.

Who uses it: Report owners, analysts, support teams.

Answers: Did the export complete? Are expected files present? Are there obvious data gaps?

Support

Pipeline Diagnostics

What it is for: Shows each data pipeline stage: raw cost, normalized region, retail join, enriched output, and audit outputs.

Why it was created: Troubleshooting requires seeing where data entered or failed in the pipeline.

Who uses it: Support engineers, analysts, developers.

Answers: Where did a row appear? Where did it stop? Which transformation changed it?

Technical

Model Technical Tables

What it is for: Shows model tables and schema-oriented support information.

Why it was created: Engineers need to understand model structure without opening every TMDL file.

Who uses it: Developers, support engineers, security reviewers.

Answers: What tables exist? What data is imported? How does the model organize report data?

Drillthrough

Granular Detail / Drillthrough

What it is for: Provides filtered row-level detail from overview selections.

Why it was created: Users often need to move from an executive number to the exact rows behind it.

Who uses it: Analysts, customer engineers, FinOps practitioners.

Answers: What exact usage rows make up this service, region, or savings value?
